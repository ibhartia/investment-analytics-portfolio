# Sentiment Scoring Rubric

Detailed criteria for scoring each sentiment dimension on a -5 to +5 scale.

## Management Tone

| Score Range | Criteria |
|------------|---------|
| +4 to +5 | Highly confident, specific, energetic delivery. Uses strong affirmatives ("we are confident", "we delivered exceptional"). Takes ownership of results. Directly addresses challenges with clear solutions. |
| +2 to +3 | Generally positive and assured. Provides concrete examples. Acknowledges challenges but frames them constructively. |
| 0 to +1 | Neutral or measured. Balanced language without strong positive or negative signals. Standard corporate tone. |
| -1 to -2 | Slightly defensive or evasive. Overuses qualifiers ("we think", "we hope"). Deflects difficult questions. Vague on specifics. |
| -3 to -4 | Notably defensive. Blames external factors repeatedly. Avoids direct answers. Tone shifts noticeably during tough questions. |
| -5 | Highly evasive or combative. Contradicts prior guidance without acknowledgment. Hostile toward analyst questions. |

### Tone Indicators to Track
- Pronoun usage: "we delivered" (ownership) vs. "it happened" (deflection)
- Qualifier density: count of hedging words per prepared remarks paragraph
- Energy shifts: does tone change between prepared remarks and Q&A?
- Repetition: repeating the same talking point may signal scripted deflection

## Forward Guidance

| Score Range | Criteria |
|------------|---------|
| +4 to +5 | Raises guidance with specific numbers. Provides new long-term targets. Uses "will" and "expect to achieve". Multiple upward revisions. |
| +2 to +3 | Reaffirms or slightly raises guidance. Provides specific ranges. Confident qualifiers around projections. |
| 0 to +1 | Maintains existing guidance. No changes. Standard outlook language. |
| -1 to -2 | Widens guidance ranges. Adds new qualifiers. Shifts from specific numbers to directional language ("improvement" instead of "15% growth"). |
| -3 to -4 | Lowers guidance. Removes prior targets. Introduces significant caveats. Shifts to "aspirational" framing. |
| -5 | Withdraws guidance entirely. Refuses to provide outlook. Signals fundamental uncertainty about near-term performance. |

### Guidance Signals
- Specificity: numbers and ranges vs. directional words
- Timeframe: near-term confidence vs. pushing targets further out
- Qualifier evolution: compare this quarter's qualifier usage to language from prior calls if available
- New metrics: introducing new non-standard metrics may signal an attempt to redirect attention

## Risk Language

| Score Range | Criteria |
|------------|---------|
| +4 to +5 | Minimal risk language. Proactively addresses potential concerns and dismisses them with evidence. Frames challenges as already resolved or manageable. |
| +2 to +3 | Acknowledges risks but frames them as contained. Provides mitigation plans with specifics. |
| 0 to +1 | Standard risk disclosures. Balanced risk/opportunity framing. |
| -1 to -2 | Elevated risk mentions. New risk categories introduced. Hedging increases around previously confident areas. |
| -3 to -4 | Frequent use of "uncertain", "challenging", "headwinds". Multiple new risk factors. Macro concerns dominate prepared remarks. |
| -5 | Pervasive risk language. Existential or structural concerns raised. Going concern language or liquidity warnings. |

### Risk Vocabulary Tiers
- **Tier 1 (mild):** "monitoring", "watching closely", "mindful of"
- **Tier 2 (moderate):** "challenging", "headwinds", "uncertainty", "volatility"
- **Tier 3 (severe):** "significant risk", "material impact", "restructuring", "liquidity concerns"

## Analyst Sentiment

| Score Range | Criteria |
|------------|---------|
| +4 to +5 | Congratulatory openings ("great quarter"). Questions focused on upside and expansion. Minimal follow-up pushback. Analysts building on positive themes. |
| +2 to +3 | Generally supportive questions. Follow-ups seek detail on growth, not skepticism. Positive framing in question setup. |
| 0 to +1 | Neutral, information-seeking questions. Standard quarterly check-in tone. |
| -1 to -2 | Pointed questions about misses or shortfalls. More follow-ups requesting clarification. Skeptical reframing of management answers. |
| -3 to -4 | Aggressive questioning. Multiple analysts on same concern. Directly challenging management's narrative. Questions about cash position or covenant compliance. |
| -5 | Hostile questioning. Demands for accountability. Questions about management changes, strategic alternatives, or going concern. |

### Analyst Behavior Patterns
- Opening salutation: "congrats on the quarter" vs. jumping straight to tough questions
- Follow-up ratio: multiple follow-ups on one topic signals dissatisfaction with the answer
- Cross-analyst convergence: multiple analysts asking about the same issue is a red flag
- Question framing: "Can you help us understand..." (neutral) vs. "Why did you..." (adversarial)

## Financial Health Signals

| Score Range | Criteria |
|------------|---------|
| +4 to +5 | Emphasizes record metrics, margin expansion, accelerating growth. Announces buybacks, dividend increases, or debt paydown. "Best quarter in company history" language. |
| +2 to +3 | Highlights beats on key metrics. Positive trajectory language. Healthy balance sheet commentary. |
| 0 to +1 | Meets expectations language. Stable metrics. No significant positive or negative signals. |
| -1 to -2 | Emphasizes "adjusted" or "non-GAAP" metrics disproportionately. Shifts focus from revenue to bookings or pipeline. Margin pressure acknowledged. |
| -3 to -4 | Misses on key metrics. Restructuring charges. Cost-cutting emphasis over growth. Debt concerns surface. |
| -5 | Cash burn warnings. Covenant risk. Going concern language. Capital raise discussions. Revenue decline acceleration. |

### Financial Language Patterns
- Metric cherry-picking: highlighting unusual metrics while downplaying standard ones
- "Excluding" language: frequency of one-time adjustments
- Cash vs. accrual emphasis: shifting to cash metrics may signal accrual accounting concerns
- Comparability: changing segments, metrics, or comparison periods

## Strategic Clarity

| Score Range | Criteria |
|------------|---------|
| +4 to +5 | Clear, specific multi-year strategy with measurable milestones. New initiatives tied to financial targets. Consistent narrative across prepared remarks and Q&A. |
| +2 to +3 | Coherent strategy with reasonable specificity. Key initiatives identified. Priorities are clear. |
| 0 to +1 | Standard strategic language. Nothing new or concerning. General directional statements. |
| -1 to -2 | Vague strategic statements. Priorities seem to shift. Buzzword-heavy with little substance. Inconsistencies between prepared remarks and Q&A answers. |
| -3 to -4 | Strategy pivot without clear rationale. Contradicts prior quarter's priorities. Leadership changes affecting strategic direction. |
| -5 | No coherent strategy articulated. Reactive posture. Fundamental business model questions unanswered. |

### Clarity Indicators
- Initiative count: too many priorities = no priorities
- Specificity: "We will launch X in Q3" vs. "We are exploring opportunities"
- Consistency: do Q&A answers align with prepared remarks?
- Follow-through: references to prior commitments and their status
